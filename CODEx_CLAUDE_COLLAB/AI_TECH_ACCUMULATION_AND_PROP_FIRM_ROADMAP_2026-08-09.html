AI Accumulation and Prop-Firm Roadmap

A capital-growth plan for a small account and a separate, evidence-gated route to autonomous futures trading.

Prepared August 9, 2026. Assumption: emergency savings are separate and invested capital has a five-year or longer horizon.

Decision

Use investing to build capital and use the bot to prove a trading edge. Do not ask the same $1,000 to provide diversification, AI upside, and weekly income. Those are conflicting jobs.

Starting Allocation Framework

60% Broad coreVTI or a comparable total-market fund. This already contains substantial technology exposure while reducing single-company failure risk.
25% AI overlay15% semiconductor ETF such as SMH or SOXX, plus 10% split between two profitable AI platforms such as GOOGL and AMZN.
15% reserveHold for scheduled future purchases, not impulsive dips. No leverage, margin, inverse ETFs, or 3x semiconductor products.

Deployment rule

Invest one quarter of the planned amount every two weeks over eight weeks. Add new money automatically to the broad core. A falling price alone is not a buy signal; the business thesis must remain intact.

Twelve-Month Capital Roadmap

  1. Weeks 1-8: Build the allocation in four equal installments. Record every purchase, thesis, and expected holding period.
  2. Monthly: Automatically add whatever amount is sustainable. Direct at least 70% of contributions to the broad core until the account exceeds $5,000.
  3. Quarterly: Review revenue growth, free cash flow, margins, AI capital intensity, guidance, and thesis disconfirmers. Do not react to daily charts.
  4. At a 20% drawdown: Recheck the thesis. Continue scheduled purchases only when fundamentals remain intact; never double position size merely because price fell.
  5. At $5,000: Permit individual stocks to reach no more than 7.5% each. Keep total semiconductor exposure visible across both ETFs and direct holdings.
  6. At $10,000: Reassess whether the AI overlay exceeds 35% of the portfolio. Rebalance with new contributions before selling taxable positions.

Why the Current Bot Is Not Prop-Ready

The current production thesis trades SPY options through Alpaca. Most retail prop evaluations trade futures. Moving the ORB signal to MES or MNQ changes tick value, session behavior, fills, volatility, and drawdown mechanics. It is a new strategy requiring new evidence.

Low risk is not enoughA positive payoff ratio still fails if the win rate, costs, or losing streak exceed assumptions.
Drawdown is path-dependentA profitable strategy can breach a trailing maximum loss before its edge appears.
Rules change behaviorConsistency, position limits, payout conditions, and prohibited conduct must be modeled as part of the strategy.

Prop-Firm Validation Roadmap

  1. Choose one ruleset: Prefer a firm that explicitly permits automation and publishes maximum-loss, consistency, payout, and prohibited-strategy rules. Do not optimize across multiple firms.
  2. Build an MES-only adapter: One micro contract, no averaging down, no overnight exposure, bracket order required, duplicate-order protection required.
  3. Backtest correctly: Use futures data, exact exchange session, commissions, at least one tick of adverse entry and exit slippage, walk-forward splits, and locked holdout data.
  4. Shadow the exact evaluation: Apply the firm's profit target, maximum loss, daily loss, consistency, and permitted hours inside the simulator.
  5. Pass three consecutive simulated evaluations: No resets, no rule exceptions, at least 100 total trades, positive net expectancy, and no single day supplying more than 30% of profits.
  6. Buy one evaluation: Risk no more than 5% of the available drawdown per trade, stop for the day after two losses, and take no more than two qualified entries.
  7. Scale after payout evidence: Do not add accounts or a trade copier until two real payouts have cleared and live slippage matches simulation.

Topstep currently says automated strategies are allowed with conditions, but it provides no exception for malfunctions. Its evaluation also uses a maximum-loss rule and a consistency target. Those constraints must exist in the bot before paying an evaluation fee.
